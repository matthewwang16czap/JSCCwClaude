# Prior work anticipates the method, not the arm

Yes. The scheduling method has been published several times under other names; the setting has not. Media streaming got there first. RaDiO (Chou and Miao, technical report 2001, IEEE TMM 2006) gives every data unit its own finite-horizon program up to its deadline and prices the shared rate with a Lagrange multiplier. In its rate-controlled form it sends the unit with the largest "threshold multiplier", an indifference-price index in all but name, whose closed-form approximation costs "a fraction of a dB" ([Chou and Miao](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ChouM06.pdf)). Fu and van der Schaar (IEEE JSAC 2010) split multi-user wireless video into per-user MDPs coordinated by resource prices ([arXiv 0903.0207](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0207)), and Hosseini and Panwar solve layered video with playback buffers as a restless bandit, plotting the value of its LP relaxation beside their policies ([arXiv 1612.03985](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03985)). Hao, Xu and Tong (IEEE TAC 2022) define the "Lagrangian priority value" as "the greatest tax under which it is optimal to activate the arm", with future prices fixed at the relaxation's optimum ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9314189)), which is both the name and the definition of your Lagrangian index. Kalman-filter, age-of-information and remote-inference scheduling run the same relax–decompose–index recipe, operations research has a name for every step, and SoftCast, ParCast and Lee–Petersen own the additive MMSE form by construction. **What no paper in the eight fields has is your arm**: a learned analog prefix code whose phases are spread over several slots and accumulate, each degraded smoothly by its own slot's SNR, which forces a two-number user state (phases held, accumulated sensitivity-weighted noise). The nearest scheduler of a learned code, McDowell et al. (2026, read in full), gives each sensor a codelength per fading slot through a Lagrangian bandit, but every slot carries a fresh, self-contained codeword. Four other things are also absent: a validated link abstraction for such a code (0.055 dB error against MIESM's 0.487 dB, per [NOTES.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/NOTES.md)); stale reports handled inside a finite-horizon arm program with the bound intact (the bound itself is the standard belief-state relaxation, which Ouyang et al. already plot under ARQ-revealed channel states); a digital baseline with CQI-table link adaptation and IR-HARQ under aged reports; and a code retrained on the channel its own scheduler creates. The paper should claim the arm, the abstraction, the measured certificate, the realistic baseline and the co-design, and present the index machinery as imported. Its scheduling section should cite RaDiO, Fu–van der Schaar, Hao–Xu–Tong, Yu–Xu–Tong, Hosseini–Panwar, Shisher et al., McDowell et al. and Brown–Smith. One caveat governs every verdict below: blocked access meant only RaDiO was read in full when this report was written, so most overlap judgments rest on abstracts. The first batch of uploaded full texts (2026-10-20: Hosseini–Panwar, Fu–van der Schaar's single-user paper, Yu–Xu–Tong, Hao–Xu–Tong and RaDiO) has since been read, and so have a second batch (McDowell et al., Shisher et al.'s JSAIT paper, DeepJSCC-l, DRJSCC and a 2026 BUPT architecture article) a third (the two Ouyang et al. papers, Ayesta, Erausquin and Jacko, Le Ny, Feron and Dahleh, and the ABG fit) and a fourth (Steiner and Shamai's layered HARQ, ProgressFTX, Hou, Borkar and Kumar, Tang et al., and Kriouile and Assaad). Their corrections are made in place below, and the readings are in `research_notes/Related work for JSCC scheduling/fulltext_readings.md`. The reading table at the end lists what remains; only its first nine rows could still move a verdict.

## Every ingredient has a precedent; no paper has the combination

The search covered eight fields and found two kinds of near neighbour. **Setting twins** schedule progressively decodable content to several users over fading channels before deadlines. Every one of them uses digital layers that either decode or fail.

In multimedia, Hosseini and Panwar's SVC bandit is the closest ([arXiv 1612.03985](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03985)). Ji, Huang, Chiang, Lafruit and Catthoor weight SVC users on an OFDM downlink by content, deadline and past results, gaining up to 6 dB over content-blind schedulers ([Princeton](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/scheduling-and-resource-allocation-for-svc-streaming-over-ofdm-do/)). Pahalawatta, Berry, Pappas and Katsaggelos build a gradient scheduler whose utilities account for received-video distortion ([EUSIPCO 2006](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2006/papers/1568982331.pdf)). Liebl, Kalman and Girod hold back well-connected users until their deadlines approach ([ICME 2005](https://web.stanford.edu/~bgirod/pdfs/LieblICME05.pdf)), which is opportunism under a deadline in heuristic form.

The other fields offer only partial twins. Analog transmission has a single-user cousin, He et al.'s progressive pseudo-analog streaming, in which every extra bandwidth share raises quality and a water-filling scheduler decides what one stream sends ([MSR](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=381044)). Information theory has Tarello, Sun, Zafer and Modiano's dynamic program for N users sharing a deadline over fading, but it minimises energy rather than distortion ([EUDL](https://eudl.eu/doi/10.1109/WIOPT.2005.28)). Semantic communication has CoCo, with per-user quality targets for an adaptive-rate deep JSCC, but on AWGN and allocated once ([arXiv 2608.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13632)). Its closest twin, read in full, is McDowell, Panebianco, Chakraborty and Sun's significance-driven semantic communication ([arXiv 2608.28441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28441)). A meta-learned VIB code gets a codelength per sensor and per i.i.d. Rayleigh slot from a Lagrangian multi-action bandit, rewarded by a per-sample task value computed from the decoder's posterior. But every slot carries a fresh, self-contained codeword of the newest observation, so nothing accumulates across slots. The horizon is infinite and discounted, CSI is perfect, and the slot values are learned by reinforcement learning (3.5 GPU-hours), for lack of a link abstraction that compresses the state. **No setting twin uses a code whose phases accumulate over slots, each graded by the SNR of the slot that carried it.**

**Method twins** relax a per-slot resource constraint, solve one small program per user or item against a price, and recombine the results with an index. They appear in nearly every field, each with its own name for the shadow price of a slot and each user's willingness to pay it:

## Learned codes inherit SoftCast's algebra only when trained for it

The form of the abstraction is not yours, and `PAPER.md` already concedes it ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). **φ(n) = n/(1+n/κ) is exactly SoftCast's per-chunk LLSE error.** Take a component of variance λ, sent with gain g over noise variance n and decoded by LLSE. Its error is λn/(g²λ+n) = (1/g²)·n/(1+n/(g²λ)), so ρ ∝ 1/g² and κ = g²λ is the component's received power. Equivalently, φ is κ times the normalised MMSE of a Gaussian at SNR κ/n.

The same form runs through the linear-analog literature. SoftCast makes the network stack behave as a linear transform, so that channel noise becomes a gradual loss of sharpness ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/72994)). Lee and Petersen's optimal linear code for vector channels keeps the largest source eigencomponents and pairs them with the channel noise eigenvalues in order ([UNM](https://digitalrepository.unm.edu/ece_etds/436)). ParCast+ sends the most important source components over the highest-gain MIMO-OFDM components, for up to 6.4 dB ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=168037)). Xiong et al. show that modelling per-chunk energy accurately is worth 3–5 dB ([pkuscholar](https://pkuscholar.onrender.com/journals/tip/XiongZWX017/)). Cui, Luo, Chen and Wu allocate power and channel uses by expected squared error under statistical CSI ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=166043)). For a linear code, adding errors across slots is just taking the trace of a block-diagonal error covariance, so overlap on the form is HIGH.

What that literature never does is use the sum as a link-to-system interface. The analog community optimises distortion directly, so **no analog or hybrid digital-analog paper found discusses EESM or MIESM**, let alone tests them. Digital system simulation still compresses per-subcarrier SNRs with EESM, as in the 5G NR PHY abstraction ([arXiv 2001.10309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10309)), and 2026 semantic preprints have started to borrow it. Jiang et al. map a semantic encoder's symbols onto OFDM subcarriers and use EESM to find "the AWGN SNR that yields the same BLER" ([arXiv 2604.23691](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23691v1)). AgentComm defines the same effective SNR ([arXiv 2604.13558](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13558v1)). Neither visibly checks EESM against its semantic metric.

Multi-user semantic allocators instead plug in a quality curve measured at a single SNR. Examples are the Alpha-Beta-Gamma (ABG) MS-SSIM fit for CNN, SCUNet, ViT and Swin encoders, used for power control over fading and for max–min allocation over OFDMA users with one subcarrier each ([arXiv 2504.21055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21055); read in full: fitted per network on CIFAR-10, one SNR per image throughout); PADC's OraNet, with 0.07–0.42 dB MAE ([IEEE TWC](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2023.3234408)); semantic spectral-efficiency tables ([arXiv 2201.06023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06023v2)); and logistic fits of DeepSC similarity ([arXiv 2209.06006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.06006)). All of these amount to your "mean-dB" rule, which cannot tell which phase met the bad slot. ABG's fitted exponent is close to 1 for three of its four networks (0.97–1.06), which makes its single-SNR curve the same family as your model at one SNR, Dsrc + Cκ/(1 + κs). The MMSE shape of a learned code's quality curve at one SNR is therefore published; the rule for slots of different SNRs is not. The closest form in the semantic literature is an outage probability of an importance-weighted distortion over Rayleigh fading, with fixed importances and no scheduler ([arXiv 2312.16057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.16057)).

The genuinely new element is that the rule depends on training. **Training with SNR that varies inside one image is established.** DRJSCC draws the SNR from U[0, 20] dB on every block ([arXiv 2311.15309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15309)). CFA-JSCC handles transmissions that "span multiple fading events" ([arXiv 2412.08211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08211)). euJSCC fine-tunes on time-varying channels ([arXiv 2602.14018](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14018)). CA-JSCC learns to give better subcarriers more power ([arXiv 2205.02417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.02417)). But each of them adapts the encoder to fed-back CSI rather than exposing per-chunk weights. None proposes a composition rule, and none reports that constant-SNR training destroys one.

Two full texts read in batch 2 come closest to that last finding and explain it. In DRJSCC, ADJSCC, trained with one SNR per image, matches the block-wise scheme only when the SNR barely changes within the image, and falls behind otherwise; DRJSCC gives PSNR for given per-block SNR profiles, but for a scheme that re-encodes from fed-back CSI, with no rule that composes the blocks. Kurka and Gündüz's DeepJSCC-l tests its layers only under one common SNR. It finds that refinement layers lose their value below the training SNR, "since the reconstructed base layer becomes significantly different from what the encoder expects based on the training data", and notes that a refinement cannot simply carry the residual, because the base reconstruction depends on the channel realisation ([arXiv 2009.12480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12480)). That is the mechanism behind your result: a code trained at one SNR codes its refinements against the base reconstruction it expects there, so its slots cannot add. Neither paper tests a composition rule, so the claim to the first test of MIESM and EESM on a learned code still stands, pending the two 2026 EESM preprints.

Your measurement shows both. On held-out profiles, **the sensitivity-weighted MMSE rule errs by 0.055 dB, against 0.487 dB for MIESM and 0.194 dB for EESM, and a code trained at constant SNR obeys no rule (0.222 dB)** ([NOTES.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/NOTES.md)). That makes contribution (1) the first test of effective-SNR mappings on a learned code, and the first evidence that training decides whether a learned code behaves like an analog one.

Information theory supplies the explanation reviewers will want. Layered codes over fading treat a layer as decoded or lost, so distortion multiplies with the decoded rate ([Tian et al.](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/160848?ln=fr); [Ng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.3099v1)). Hybrid digital-analog refinement multiplies distortion too. Your phases instead add, as parallel components do in Lee and Petersen's setting with "subchannel" read as "slot". The I-MMSE identity, dI/dsnr = mmse/2 for any input ([Guo, Shamai and Verdú](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0412108)), means that MI-type mappings integrate the functional an analog code obeys instead of using it directly. And with perfect CSI across K slots, ideal separation achieves the geometric mean of 1/(1+s_k), while a linear code achieves only the arithmetic mean. The analog code thus pays an AM–GM penalty for SNR spread, which is why mapping phases to slots matters for it at all.

One reviewer objection is predictable: "train a network on per-slot SNRs." OraNet, SCAN ([arXiv 2306.15534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15534)) and a CSI-feedback performance evaluator ([arXiv 2302.13477](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13477)) already do this for a single SNR. One comparison against such a predictor would answer it.

## Per-user programs under a slot price predate the restless-bandit label

### RaDiO and Fu–van der Schaar priced media units before the bandit framing

**RaDiO is the earliest verified ancestor of your scheduler** (read in full, and again in batch 1). Chou and Miao (MSR report, February 2001; IEEE TMM 2006) minimise expected distortion under an expected-rate constraint ([Chou and Miao](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ChouM06.pdf)). Every data unit gets its own finite-horizon MDP over its transmission opportunities before its deadline, priced by a Lagrange multiplier and re-solved at every opportunity. The authors call this factorisation "one of the main contributions", because the joint MDP is intractable beyond two units and two opportunities.

Their rate-controlled variant computes, for each unit, the multiplier λ_l "above which the data unit is not transmitted, and below which" it is. It then sends the unit with the largest λ_l: **an indifference-price index in all but name**. The variant approximates λ_l in closed form at "very little penalty (a fraction of a dB)" and about one percent of a 700 MHz CPU. Its test code is even a prefix code: twelve sequentially dependent units per group of frames of an embedded audio stream, each labelled with its distortion decrement, for gains of 2–6 dB.

The mapping onto your frame is direct:

| RaDiO | Your frame |
|---|---|
| data unit | phase |
| transmission opportunity | slot |
| Lagrange multiplier λ (one per stream; rate control re-sets it at each opportunity so that one unit is sent) | per-slot price (optimised jointly over the frame) |
| send the unit with the largest λ_l | Whittle index |
| closed-form λ_l | advantage index |

The departures are what the paper should state. RaDiO's losses are i.i.d., with no channel state per opportunity, so it has no opportunism. A unit arrives or it does not, rather than being graded by SNR. One client consumes one sender's rate, instead of K users contesting slots. Units are coupled through a dependency DAG by iterative sensitivity adjustment, which reaches only a local optimum. And its bound is the operational D(R) at erasure capacity: valid for every system, but blind to deadlines and causality, so not a relaxation bound on causal schedulers. One more point from the full text bears on stale reports: acknowledgements arrive after a random round trip, and each unit's MDP runs over action–observation histories that include unacknowledged sends. A per-unit program acting on delayed information is therefore old; RaDiO's information is about delivery, though, not about the channel.

The multi-user, fading version of the idea moved into price-coordinated MDPs. **Fu and van der Schaar (IEEE JSAC 2010) model multi-user wireless video as one MDP with heterogeneous traffic, time-varying channels and coupling between users. They split it into "per-user local MDPs" by Lagrangian relaxation and let a coordinator set resource prices**, with online learning when statistics are unknown ([arXiv 0903.0207](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0207)). Decomposing multi-user wireless video into per-user programs is therefore prior art and must not be claimed. The abstract leaves three things open: whether the horizon is finite, whether prices are per slot, and whether the dual value is reported as a bound. Batch 1 brought arXiv 0812.1244 instead, the same authors' single-user paper, "Decomposition Principles and Online Learning in Cross-Layer Optimization for Delay-Sensitive Applications". There a resource price and neighbour "impact factors" split one stream's data units into per-unit problems, with an online value function about 0.5 dB below full knowledge. It has no multi-user coupling, index or bound, so the multi-user verdict above still rests on the abstract of 0903.0207.

Three more media works are relevant. Huang, Li, Chiang and Katsaggelos pair optimisation decomposition and pricing with frame deadlines ([Princeton](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/joint-source-adaptation-and-resource-allocation-for-multi-user-wi/)). Chakareski and Frossard (IEEE TMM 2006) share one channel across streams in proportion to packet importance, gaining up to 6 dB from bandwidth adaptation and 10 dB from loss adaptation ([NJIT](https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/rate-distortion-optimized-distributed-packet-scheduling-of-multip/)). Hosseini and Panwar, already in the survey, are the explicit restless-bandit version for SVC: their LP solution admits up to twice as many users at a given quality and favours users with better channels and emptier buffers ([arXiv 1612.03985](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1612.03985v2)). The LP of a restless bandit is itself the Whittle relaxation. **The full text settles the question this report left open: Hosseini and Panwar solve the relaxation ("RB") as an LP and plot its value beside QAA, BEAS, PF and two buffer or channel rules for 20 users, calling it "the optimum"** (their Figs. 10–13). Layered media therefore already has a relaxation value as a numerical benchmark. Theirs is infinite-horizon and discounted, with one averaged budget rather than per-slot prices. It is never presented as a bound on causal schedulers, and never studied against the number of users.

### Operations research and deadline-bandit theory already name your bound and your index

Every step of the pipeline has a standard operations-research name.

**The bound.** The per-slot prices and the bound (1−p)B·Σ_t λ_t + Σ_k W_k(λ) are the Lagrangian relaxation of a weakly coupled DP with time-indexed multipliers. Hawkins's MIT thesis already applies it to restless bandits ([DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/29599)), and Adelman and Mersereau compare it with approximate LPs ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1070.0445)).

**The Lagrangian index** goes by several names. It is Hawkins's per-period recombination step, which with one cardinality constraint per slot reduces to sorting. It is Bertsimas and Niño-Mora's LP-dual "primal-dual index" ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v48y2000i1p80-90.html)). It is Brown and Smith's Lagrangian index policy, for which tie-breaking and mixing "matter a great deal" ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3342)). And it is the LP-index or fluid-priority policy of finite-horizon asymptotics ([Hu and Frazier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00205v1); [Zhang and Frazier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11911v1); [Gast, Gaujal and Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10067v3)).

**Revenue management** names the prices too. λ*_t is an open-loop bid price for a perishable slot, and your rule is a bid-price policy with priority rationing, since it always fills every unit. Brown and Zhang's "fluid-budget balancing" policies, within O(1) of optimal under non-degeneracy, formalise "use every free unit" ([Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1559343)).

The two closest deadline-bandit papers are a problem twin and a method twin.

**Yu, Xu and Tong (IEEE TAC 2018) are the problem twin.** They schedule jobs with deadlines under a cap on simultaneous processing, with convex non-completion penalties that act as a concave terminal reward of accumulated service. The problem is indexable and has a closed-form Whittle index for constant costs ([arXiv 1610.00399](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1610.00399v4)). The full text qualifies the scaling result: the horizon is infinite and discounted, and the gap vanishes as processors are added only in light traffic, with Poisson arrivals of mean μ < M/e, where the capacity rarely binds. Their gap bound comes from comparing with the uncapacitated policy, and the bound in their plots is the single-price Whittle relaxation. Their Whittle index is the smallest subsidy at which resting is optimal, the bottom of any indifference range. Their service per slot is a fixed unit, which is exactly why your arm needs a second state coordinate.

**Hao, Xu and Tong (IEEE TAC 2022) are the method twin, and the full text makes them the source of your Lagrangian index.** They relax the problem into single-arm programs with one multiplier per stage, solve the dual exactly as an LP, and define the "Lagrangian priority value" as **"the greatest tax under which it is optimal to activate the arm"**, with the future multipliers fixed at the relaxation's optimum. Its closed form is the advantage of acting at those prices, which is what your `gain_at` computes. They prove asymptotic optimality in light and heavy traffic and beat the Whittle index by 22–49% when per-stage capacity varies ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9314189); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10250769)).

Two details of the full text matter more than the headline. First, the theorem needs a randomized tie rule drawn from the relaxation's optimal policy: a tied arm moves up with the probability that the relaxation activates its state. With uniform ties, the same priority stays below the randomized version, which reaches the bound, at every size up to 4,000 arms: 6–8% below in heavy overload, and further below in slight overload (their Figs. 3–4). Second, the "top of the indifference range" convention in your docs ([PROBLEM.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PROBLEM.md)) is not theirs in substance. With future prices fixed, the gain falls with the current tax at slope −1, so their supremum is over a single point. For a constant-subsidy Whittle index, where the range is real, Yu, Xu and Tong take the bottom, and so do Le Ny, Feron and Dahleh, Ouyang et al., and Kriouile and Assaad, who all define the index as an infimum. The convention therefore stays your choice, a deliberate departure from the standard definition. Finally, their margin over Whittle comes mostly from capacity that varies over time, which a constant subsidy cannot see. With constant capacity per slot, your +2.8 points over the Whittle index at the target is the small margin their analysis predicts.

The recipe recurs across wireless networks, estimation and goal-oriented communication, which makes it an import rather than an invention.

In **wireless networks**, Singh and Kumar decompose deadline-constrained networks, through the Lagrangian of a constrained MDP, into per-packet problems with transmission prices ([arXiv 1606.01608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01608); journal version IEEE TAC 64(1), 2019). Hou, Borkar and Kumar's frame model drops packets that miss the end of the frame, as yours does, but enforces long-run delivery ratios through debts ([IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/105587); read in full: largest-debt-first policies with lexicographic ties are feasibility optimal, by Blackwell's approachability theorem).

In **estimation**, Le Ny, Feron and Dahleh's Kalman-filter scheduling is **the precedent for proving a relaxation bound tight** ([arXiv 0810.5148](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.5148); read in full). They schedule N systems with M sensors in continuous time. With scalar systems and identical sensors the bound is Whittle's relaxation with one multiplier, its dual and the Whittle indices in closed form; in general it is a semidefinite program from Jensen's inequality on time averages, decomposed with one multiplier per sensor. Periodic switching built from a Birkhoff–von Neumann decomposition of the optimal sensor shares comes within O(ε) of the bound for switching period ε, so the bound is tight. In their one example, Whittle's index policy scores 7.98, on the bound, against 9.2 for greedy. Tightness comes from time-sharing in a deterministic problem: the covariance of a Kalman filter does not depend on the measured values. A frame of integral phases over stochastic slots cannot time-share, so there the gap can only be measured. Ornee and Sun give an exact Whittle index for weighted MSE over parallel channels ([arXiv 2305.04809](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.04809v4)).

In **age of information**, Tang, Wang, Song and Song run your pipeline ([arXiv 1912.05947](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05947v3); read in full). They relax a hard per-slot cap into a time average, which gives a lower bound, and decouple it with one multiplier into single-sensor constrained MDPs, each with its own power budget. The optimal single-sensor policy is a threshold in the age for each Markov channel state, solved exactly by an LP over occupation measures. They truncate it by letting every sensor draw its action from its relaxed policy and, when more sensors want the slot than it has room for, **picking among them uniformly at random**, which they prove O(1/√N) from the optimum. The lower bound is plotted against N, and the gap shrinks with N.

In **goal-oriented communication**, Shisher, Ji, Hou and Sun (IEEE JSAIT 2023, read in full) relax a per-slot channel budget into a time-average one and split it into per-source average-cost MDPs, with λ* found by stochastic subgradient ascent ([arXiv 2308.10094](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10094)). They rank by a "net gain", the advantage of sending each feature length at λ* (their eq. 65), maximised every slot as a knapsack. Their Fig. 8 plots the relaxation's lower bound beside the policy as sources and channels grow together, and the gap nearly closes once the system is three times its smallest size (read from the plot). Their channel is a count of resource units with no fading. Shisher, Sun and Hou ("Timely communications for remote inference", IEEE/ACM ToN 32(5), 2024) prove asymptotic optimality, for a multi-action bandit, of a policy that combines Whittle-index selection with duality ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TNET.2024.3408673)). McDowell et al. (read in full) keep the knapsack, add an LP-priority tie rule ("Q-Maximization", from a 2026 manuscript by Chakraborty, Sun, Hou and Eryilmaz), and report, citing that manuscript, that the net-gain knapsack with arbitrary ties is not asymptotically optimal.

For **single users**, Fu, Modiano and Tsitsiklis solve the finite-horizon deadline DP over fading in two cases: when the channel state is known before transmitting (your perfect-CQI case), and when it is learned only afterwards ([MIT](https://stuff.mit.edu/people/jnt/Papers/C-02-fu-wrlss-CISS.pdf)). Derman, Lieberman and Ross assign weighted items to slots of random value that arrive in sequence, using thresholds that do not depend on the weights ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v18y1972i7p349-355.html)). At price zero and with free phase order, your per-user problem is exactly that assignment. The prefix order, the choice of length and the price are what break its structure and make your DP necessary.

**Information theory** has no multi-user scheduler that minimises distortion before a deadline. Its nearest structure is progressive transmission, which places one layer per fading block without knowing the blocks' SNRs ([Gündüz and Erkip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1161)).

What remains yours in contribution (2) is the arm and the evidence, not the machinery.

**The arm.** In every age-of-information and Kalman index, a passive arm gets worse on its own, because age or covariance grows. Tripathi and Modiano show that estimation error then reduces to a function of age ([arXiv 1908.10438](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10438)). Your passive arm is frozen; it is restless only through its channel report and the deadline clock. Each delivery is a partial refinement whose value depends on which phase it carries. So no age-penalty index can represent it, and the index can be zero for a user far from done.

**The evidence.** A relaxation value plotted beside the policies is standard practice, as the full texts show: Hosseini and Panwar plot it at 20 users, Yu, Xu and Tong at 10 to 1,000 queue positions, Hao, Xu and Tong at 50 to 4,000 arms, Shisher et al. against a growing system scale, Ouyang, Eryilmaz and Shroff for 5 to 500 downlink users, where Whittle's index policy meets the bound by 50, and Tang et al. for 5 to 50 sensors. Asymptotic theorems, RaDiO's capacity bound, Le Ny et al.'s proof of tightness and Ouyang et al.'s brute-force optimum on five users certify the rest. What is new is the arm the bound is computed for, and how it is used: per-slot prices over a finite frame, a decoded codec reward, confidence intervals and a scaling study. Your certificate puts the closed form within 0.26 dB of every causal scheduler at K = 8, and the Lagrangian index within 0.005 dB per user at K = 128 with B = K/8 ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). That is new evidence of a standard kind, not a new method.

**Learned schedulers.** Deep-RL semantic schedulers report no bound at all, so your bound also caps what they could add on your problem ([arXiv 2505.23249](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23249); [Zhang et al., TCOM 2023](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/98084)). Learned-index work targets unknown models: Ceran, Gündüz and György, for example, learn age-of-information policies under HARQ when channel statistics are unknown ([arXiv 2102.09774](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09774)). Your model is known, so exact indices and bounds are computable. Learning is complementary, useful only under model mismatch.

### Theory explains your scaling numbers and flags two of them

Three results from this literature bear directly on the numbers in `PROBLEM.md`.

**The measured rate fits the non-degenerate finite-horizon regime.** The Lagrangian index's total gap, K times the per-user gap, falls from 1.66 dB at K = 8 to 0.64 dB at K = 128 ([PROBLEM.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PROBLEM.md)). It stays bounded rather than growing like √K, which matches Zhang and Frazier's O(1) result for fluid-priority policies under non-degeneracy ([arXiv 2107.11911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11911v1)).

**The bound is as tight as any first-order relaxation, but probably loose at K = 8.** Brown and Zhang prove that the approximate-LP bound equals the Lagrangian bound when subproblem actions are binary and the linking constraints are unimodular ([SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3849573)); a per-slot cardinality row satisfies both. Yet lost slots are a signal shared by every user, and Brown and Zhang's Operations Research paper shows that multipliers blind to the signal's history can leave a gap linear in the number of arms ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v70y2022i5p3015-3033.html)). At K = 8 the clairvoyant optimum is the tighter cap (+0.23 dB, against the Lagrangian bound's +0.26 dB) ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)), which fits that looseness. Prices conditioned on the slots lost so far, or an information relaxation with Lagrangian penalties ([Ye, Zhu and Zhou](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.3363v1)), would tighten it.

**The 30 dB target result conflicts with the theory as stated.** The Lagrangian index stays about 1.5 points from the bound at every K ([PROBLEM.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PROBLEM.md)), whereas the LP-index policy loses only O(1/√K) per arm on every finite model ([Gast, Gaujal and Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10067v3)). A gap that does not shrink means one of two things. Either the computed bound is not the policy's own LP, through grid interpolation of a 0/1 reward or unconverged prices, or ties decide the outcome in the flat value regions that threshold rewards create, the degenerate case where Brown and Smith warn that tie-breaking dominates. This should be diagnosed, ideally by solving the discretised LP exactly, before the target result is called near-optimal.

The batch-1 full texts sharpen the diagnosis. Hao, Xu and Tong show the symptom itself: their Lagrangian priority with uniform ties stays a constant fraction below the bound as the number of arms grows to 4,000, while the version with the relaxation's randomized ties converges. Batch 2 adds the multi-action counterpart: McDowell et al., citing a 2026 manuscript by Chakraborty, Sun, Hou and Eryilmaz, report that the per-slot knapsack with arbitrary ties is not asymptotically optimal, and that keeping the larger codelength among tied maximisers at the relaxation's mixed-action states makes it so. Batch 4 adds the contrast that isolates the cause. Tang et al. resolve conflicts uniformly at random among the users whose relaxed policy wants the slot, and prove that asymptotically optimal for age of information, because a deferred sensor waits one more slot at a bounded cost and keeps asking. Under a deadline with an all-or-nothing target, a deferred user can lose its last good slot; that is where Hao, Xu and Tong's uniform ties stay below their bound, and where your random-guided rule sits 3.1 points from the bound at K = 128, against 1.5 for the Lagrangian index. The tie rule should come from the relaxation's own policy, as Hao, Xu and Tong's does. With fresh reports your decision SNR is continuous, so exact ties are rare; they become structural only on stale reports and in the flat regions of a 0/1 reward. A check of the price solver found the other suspect. Its subgradient step grows with the unit imbalance, and at B = 4 and 8 (the sizes checked) the per-slot refinement never improved on the best constant price. For mean PSNR that costs only 0.007 dB per user on the smoke stand-in. The stand-in's 30 dB budget does not bind, so the target case must be run on the real utility. A COMMANDS section can separate the suspects with four runs: supply-scaled steps, a tie rule drawn from the relaxation, finer fade bins in the programs, and frames drawn from the programs' own discrete channel.

### The advantage index is a certainty-equivalent cousin of PI and proportional fair

Ranking users by "this slot's value against a typical alternative" is already published, in ratio form, for flow-level costs. **Ayesta, Erausquin and Jacko's Potential Improvement (PI) index divides the current service rate by its expected potential improvement** ([Lancaster](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/65375/); read in full). Exactly, ν^PI = c_k μ_{k,n} / Σ_{m>n} q_{k,m}(μ_{k,m} − μ_{k,n}): the completion probability in the current channel state over the expected improvement of the next slot over this one, infinite in the best state, which gets absolute priority (ties by cμ). It is the time-average, holding-cost member of a family of Whittle indices for geometric jobs over i.i.d. channel states; other members approximate Relatively Best (≈ PF) and Proportionally Best for long jobs, so those rules are relatives of PI rather than special cases. PI reduces to the cμ-rule at constant rates and achieves the maximum stability region; in simulations it is at least as good as the best of RB, PB, a score-based rule and cμ. No bound is reported. Jacko's Gilbert–Elliott extension compares a one-period with a steady-state improvement ([Lancaster](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/65374/)). Aalto, Lassila and Osti benchmark a size-aware Whittle index against both PF and PI ([slides](https://www.netlab.tkk.fi/~samuli/Presentations/Conferences/aalto-sigmetrics-2015.pdf)).

Content-times-channel ranking is old in video (Pahalawatta et al. and Ji et al., above) and newer in semantic edge learning. There, Liu, Zhu, Zhang and Huang's data-importance indicator combines a device's SNR with the uncertainty of its data ([arXiv 1910.02214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.02214)), and importance ARQ uses a threshold of SNR against uncertainty ([arXiv 1812.02030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02030)). In control, a value-of-information priority plays the same role ([Molin, Esen and Johansson](https://puma.ub.uni-stuttgart.de/bibtex/1694dafbc846f5aa65e85853470e033bd)). RaDiO's closed-form threshold multiplier is the media precedent for replacing a program's index with a formula at "a fraction of a dB" of cost ([Chou and Miao](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ChouM06.pdf)).

Operations research names your rule precisely and predicts how it behaves. The quotas are certainty-equivalent planning: replace future SNRs by their mean, then solve the separable concave allocation by equal slopes. The advantage index is a one-step lookahead whose value-to-go is the certainty-equivalent value of one typical slot; it is the "advantage" Q − V, with V approximated by certainty equivalence. Gocgun and Ghate find that Lagrangian decisions beat myopic ones by a statistically significant margin in weakly coupled scheduling MDPs ([UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/lagrangian-relaxation-and-constraint-generation-for-allocation-an/)). Brown and Zhang's guarantees cover policies that follow the fluid relaxation, not certainty-equivalent plans ([Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1559343)).

The reason is specific to your problem. The fluid relaxation averages across users but keeps each user's own fading, and with it the option value of waiting for a good slot. Certainty equivalence discards both, and a user's own fading never averages out as K grows. **That is exactly the measured plateau: the per-user gap falls only from 0.267 to 0.155 dB, so the total gap rises from about 2.1 dB at K = 8 to 19.8 dB at K = 128, and PF behaves the same way** ([PROBLEM.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PROBLEM.md)).

Three parts are new: the difference form, in terminal-PSNR units, against the user's own mean-SNR slot inside a deadline frame; the quotas; and this diagnosis. The paper should present the closed form as the certainty-equivalent approximation of the Lagrangian index; replacing the typical-slot value-to-go with λ*_t turns one into the other. It should also add a PI-type ratio baseline, which flow-level reviewers will expect. For your arm it reads ν_k(s) = g_k(s) / E[(g_k(S') − g_k(s))⁺], with g_k(s) the predicted PSNR gain of user k's next phase at slot SNR s and S' drawn from k's own fading distribution.

### Admission and online stopping have only single-link or long-run ancestors

Quality targets and stopping rules are common; combining them with a shared slot budget and a deadline is not.

On one link, Lan, Zeng, Popovski, Gündüz and Huang's ProgressFTX sends features until a target confidence is reached, and stops once another feature's uncertainty reduction no longer pays for its communication cost ([arXiv 2112.07244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.07244); read in full). Its transmitter does not see the fading (a fixed rate with outage), so there is nothing to time. Because the gain of its most-important-first order is concave in the number of slots, the optimal stopping rule is a one-step threshold, with the per-slot cost scaled by 1/(1 − p_o) under outage. Multi-device scheduling is named as future work. JSCCformer-f stops at a target PSNR using channel-output feedback ([arXiv 2306.09101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09101)). PADC gives each image the least rate that meets a PSNR floor ([IEEE TWC](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2023.3234408)).

Across users, Hou, Borkar and Kumar test whether a set of long-run delivery requirements is feasible, in nearly linear time, for admission control ([IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/105587)): N nested checks after sorting the clients by their required ratios, O(Nτ²) in all for frames of τ slots. Hosseini and Panwar count users admitted at a quality. CoCo meets per-user quality targets on AWGN ([arXiv 2608.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13632)). A delay-constrained video study finds that enforcing equal quality cuts the number of users supported, because cell-edge users consume more resources ([arXiv 1311.5921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5921)). Ayesta, Jacko and Novak treat abandonment, a random deadline with an all-or-nothing penalty ([Lancaster](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/71356)).

Admitting the smallest remaining needs and stopping each user online has no verified precedent. The comparison with Hou, Borkar and Kumar exposes one limitation: their guarantees are per client and across frames, while yours maximise each frame's share. If the same hard users persisted from frame to frame, admission by smallest need could starve them; a per-user debt carried across frames is the standard remedy. Your simulations redraw users every frame, so they cannot show starvation, and the paper should say so. Neither does the finding that, on stale reports, stopping must use the posterior plus a 0.5–1 dB margin ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). The caution about ties in the previous subsection applies here with full force. Hong's analysis of all-or-nothing rewards, already in the survey, shows random tie-breaking failing for the same reason ([CMU blog](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~csd-phd-blog/2026/restless-bandits/)).

## Stale reports, delay and the digital baseline are known axes, never combined

### Partially observed arms are standard; a report kernel inside a deadline program is not

Wireless restless bandits have handled imperfect channel knowledge for fifteen years, under a different observation model.

Ouyang, Murugesan, Eryilmaz and Shroff map downlink scheduling over two-state Markov channels, whose state a scheduled user reveals at the end of the slot through ARQ, to a restless bandit with a closed-form Whittle index ([arXiv 1009.3959](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.3959); read in full). Their low state supports a reduced rate, so a scheduled user's channel is first estimated and its rate adapted. Against the exact optimum, found by brute force for four or five users, the index policy captures 90–100% of what the feedback is worth. With no channel memory the feedback is worthless and the index policy is uniform random scheduling, the published counterpart of your r = 0 limit, where the index becomes the user's expected marginal gain. Ouyang, Eryilmaz and Shroff (read in full) relax the per-slot cap of a two-class ON/OFF version to a long-run average ([arXiv 1108.3768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.3768)). The relaxed policy's per-user throughput is "a per-user throughput performance upper bound to all policies" under that information structure, and Whittle's index policy reaches it as the number of users grows: locally by a fluid limit, globally under a hitting-time assumption that they check numerically. **Plotted against N, the policy meets the bound by 50 users.** Aalto, Lassila and Taboada derive an explicit Whittle index under partial channel information, where indexability holds ([Aaltodoc](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/4f9064e8-d4b0-4b81-a048-e1a3180d5e40)).

In age-of-information work, Chen and Ephremides use a Whittle index under imperfect CSI when the problem is indexable, and an "indexed priority policy" otherwise. They note that **with CSI a Whittle policy is "generally infeasible"** ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/23/12/1572)). This is the counterpart of your finding: on stale reports the Whittle index degrades (0.15 dB below the closed form at r = 0.4), while the Lagrangian index, which needs no indexability, stays best ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). Kriouile and Assaad derive closed-form Whittle indices for a POMDP over beliefs, through a Lagrangian threshold dual ([arXiv 2102.03245](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03245)). The full text shows that the belief is over the sources' states, while the channel is an i.i.d. erasure with no CSI, so theirs is not an imperfect-CSI precedent.

The analog literature makes the encoder robust instead of the scheduler. It uses statistical-CSI allocation, worth about 2 dB in average and 5 dB in outage PSNR over SoftCast ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=166043)); power allocation from channel prediction, in A-HDAVT ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7123183/authors)); and water-filling that accounts for CSI noise, for hybrid digital-analog video over OFDM ([SPIC record](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0923-5965/volumes-and-issues/257/paper-detail/40785)). Information theory quantifies how quickly graded coding stops paying as transmitter CSI improves: with more than one feedback bit in Rayleigh fading, the gain from broadcast-style layering becomes negligible ([Steiner and Shamai](https://ece.technion.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/publication_663-1.pdf)).

So a relaxation bound that holds for every scheduler seeing only partial channel information is standard for belief-state restless bandits, and has been plotted against N in a wireless downlink. What is yours is the model it is applied to. Every user reports every slot, so reports carry no exploration value. Fading is i.i.d. across slots, so the belief is a one-step Gaussian kernel, h | report ~ CN(r·h_rep, 1 − r²) with Jakes correlation, rather than a Markov belief. The horizon is finite, with a terminal quality reward. And the Lagrangian bound stays valid for every scheduler that sees only reports, by the same belief-state argument (an information relaxation in Brown, Smith and Sun's sense, [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1090.0796)), now with the report kernel inside each finite-horizon program.

Two theory links strengthen the section. First, Gast, Gaujal and Yan find that re-solving the LP at every epoch beats the static LP-index policy when the model is misestimated ([arXiv 2106.10067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10067v3)). That suggests re-solving λ*_t within the frame when the report kernel is uncertain. It also explains why a Whittle index computed under one constant price path is the fragile one. Second, a short derivation from the Gaussian kernel shows that the multiuser selection gain is scaled by r². Picking the best of K unit-mean Rayleigh reports yields an expected actual gain of r²·H_K + (1 − r²), where H_K is the K-th harmonic number. **Your measured retention of the scheduler-made lift, 65% at r = 0.8 and 8% at r = 0.3 ([PROBLEM.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PROBLEM.md)), sits almost exactly on r² (0.64 and 0.09).** The paper should state this match once the classical citation on outdated feedback is located.

### Delay metrics exist everywhere except for scheduled learned codes

Each delay metric you measure is standard on its own. Continuous traffic with per-request deadlines is Yu, Xu and Tong's model, which `stream_sim` already instantiates with your reward ([arXiv 1610.00399](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1610.00399v4)). Frames that drop undelivered packets are Hou, Borkar and Kumar's ([IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/105587)). On one link, ProgressFTX's "average communication latency", the mean number of slots to reach a target accuracy, is the form of your time to the target ([arXiv 2112.07244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.07244)). Threshold policies for live streaming under stringent deadlines are Zhao and Lin's ([INFOCOM 2014](https://jhc.sjtu.edu.cn/~shizhenzhao/infocom2014.pdf)).

Semantic communication has its own versions. CoCo minimises latency under per-user quality targets, cutting it 8–18% against rate-only adaptation on AWGN ([arXiv 2608.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13632)). A "semantic deadline" bounds when conditioning data must arrive ([arXiv 2508.12701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12701)). Liu, Guo et al. maximise task success under a delay constraint, with user selection ([arXiv 2204.08910](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2204.08910)). A queueing analysis treats the latent dimension as a latency–fidelity control over block Rayleigh fading ([arXiv 2605.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05514)).

And information theory shows that a delay constraint on the source is what forces a DP onto the distortion objective ([Holliday, Goldsmith and Poor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.3702)).

None of these measures delay for a learned code scheduled to a deadline, so the new content is your evidence ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). ### No image JSCC comparison has put IR-HARQ and CQI tables in the digital chain

The usual deep-JSCC-versus-digital margins come from digital baselines that cannot absorb CSI error. Yang, Bian and Kim give their separation baseline perfect CSI, and note that it falls more than 3 dB behind at the same rate once its CSI is imperfect ([arXiv 2101.03909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.03909)). An OFDM-guided study reports a 2.5–4 dB SNR gain at equal quality ([arXiv 2109.05194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05194)). DeepJSCC-f grants separation perfect receiver CSI and capacity-achieving codes ([Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/research-centres-and-groups/ipc-lab/KurkaGunduz_deepJSCC-f.pdf)). Quad-DeepSC sweeps 5G MCS in Sionna, with no retransmissions ([arXiv 2512.05395](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05395v1)). A 2026 driving study shows BPG + LDPC collapsing to near 0 dB when decoding fails, with neither link adaptation nor HARQ ([arXiv 2604.20278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20278)).

The analog literature's 5–8 dB margins over H.264 come mostly from broadcast, where digital must pick one rate for heterogeneous receivers ([SoftCast](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/72994); [LayerCast](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/layered-soft-video-broadcast-for-heterogeneous-receivers/)). Its closest unicast baseline, robust rate adaptation with H.264 SVC, trails by up to 5.3 dB ([Cui et al., JSTSP 2015](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=168039)). Semantic-HARQ papers compare semantic against traditional HARQ for text and features, not an analog image code against a CQI-table chain ([SemHARQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08490); [Jiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03009)).

**The one close study is Mohajer, Bavaghar, Mirzaei and Fernando (Wireless Networks 2026).** It characterises when analog JSCC beats separation with outer-loop link adaptation under imperfect transmitter CSI, and reports up to about 76% lower mean distortion on fading traces ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11276-026-04165-9)). But it uses a task metric, works by analysis and RL, and does not mention HARQ in its abstract.

Your comparison is new in kind. It pits the code against HEVC intra with LTE CQI-table link adaptation and incremental-redundancy HARQ, under CQI errors and Jakes-aged reports, inside a multi-user scheduler. The margins are 1.3–1.7 dB and grow with report age, from +0.3 dB on fresh reports ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). The smaller size is a feature: with broadcast's single-rate penalty gone, the comparison isolates the CSI-error problem.

Theory predicts the direction of the result but not its size, and it names the stronger competitor a referee may demand. Distortion-exponent results favour graded codes over fixed-rate separation when the transmitter lacks the channel state ([Laneman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0412113); [Gündüz and Erkip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1161); [Caire and Narayanan](https://www.infona.pl/resource/bwmeta1.element.ieee-art-000004276911)). The AM–GM argument above says ideal digital wins with perfect CSI, which fits your +0.3 dB near-tie on fresh reports. A 2026 BUPT case study, read in full, measures the same thing on one AWGN link. With the SNR known exactly, BPG + 5G NR LDPC with adaptive modulation and coding beats SwinJSCC from 7 dB up at a 30 dB target, and at CBR 1/8 and 13 dB reaches about 46 dB against SwinJSCC's 38 ([arXiv 2609.02924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02924)). It models no CSI error, which is where your lead comes from. And SNR-independent JSCC codes provably fall well short of the optimal diversity–fidelity trade-off, which is the encoder-side cost of robustness ([Taherzadeh and Poor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.5950)).

Under stale CSI, the strongest digital competitor in theory looked like layered superposition coding with HARQ per layer: Steiner and Shamai's broadcast chase-combining and incremental-redundancy schemes ([Technion](https://ece.technion.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/publication_660-1.pdf)). The full text, read in batch 4, narrows that. The schemes are designed for no transmit CSI, and their gains need a channel that stays fixed between rounds, so that the first round's feedback reveals the gain, and few retransmissions. Two-level coding nearly matches single-level IR once four rounds are allowed, and at high SNR conventional HARQ with ten rounds nearly matches broadcast HARQ's throughput, at more than three times its delay. Your digital chain has CQI, retransmits in newly faded slots and allows four IR rounds, which is the argument for omitting it. The caveat is your operating range: at 0–15 dB mean SNR, layered HARQ gains most (about 3 dB over the outage approach in their Fig. 5). The omission is therefore safest at r ≥ 0.8 and least safe at r ≤ 0.6, where a two-layer superposition baseline with an ideal successively refinable source is the check to run if a referee asks.

Caire and Tuninetti's information-theoretic HARQ model ([EURECOM](https://www.eurecom.fr/en/node/4906931)) also suggests a clean way to present your DP state. An IR-HARQ decoder succeeds once the accumulated mutual information exceeds the rate: Steiner and Shamai state the rule in exactly this form (their eq. 6), citing Caire and Tuninetti (IEEE TIT 47(5):1971–1988, 2001), and it is the rule your digital chain implements. Your state is the analog counterpart: accumulated sensitivity-weighted MMSE.

## Encode-once is crowded; scheduler-made training is empty

### Prior work settles the nesting penalty, not the system verdict

The codec mechanism behind contribution (6) is thoroughly published, much of it already in the survey. PADC trains one DeepJSCC-V for every rate by keeping a prefix of channels, and finds it on par with four per-rate models ([IEEE TWC](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2023.3234408)). DeepJSCC-MIMO's truncate-and-zero-pad mode loses at most 0.6 dB against per-M models ([arXiv 2309.00470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00470)). JSCCformer-f's variable-rate mode pays a nesting penalty of 0.58–1.73 dB at its last block ([arXiv 2306.09101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09101)). Kurka and Gündüz's DeepJSCC-l (read in full) loses almost nothing to layering on CIFAR-10 with the original DeepJSCC network, up to 16 layers. On Kodak with a larger network, two layers trail one transmission of the same total length by about 0.4 dB at 1 dB SNR and about 1.7 dB at 25 dB (their Fig. 8, read from the plot) ([arXiv 2009.12480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12480)). **Its multiple-decoder architecture, one encoder with one decoder per number of layers received, is exactly your encode-once design with a decoder per length.** Its single-decoder variant trains a prefix by masking random tails ("structured dropout"), and on CIFAR-10 the two perform alike. Other decoders specialised per budget exist in AdaTok's LoRA heads ([arXiv 2606.07185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07185)) and DeepJSCC-l++'s ratio conditioning ([arXiv 2305.13161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13161)).

Other fields have the same mechanism. The analog side has progressive streams whose every share improves quality ([He et al.](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=381044)), and layer prefixes that each receiver decodes according to its bandwidth ([LayerCast](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/layered-soft-video-broadcast-for-heterogeneous-receivers/)). Multimedia has scheduled embedded bitstreams under deadlines since RaDiO.

What separates your design is that it encodes once and needs no feedback. DRJSCC and CFA-JSCC re-encode the remaining symbols from fed-back CSI ([arXiv 2311.15309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15309); [arXiv 2412.08211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08211)), and JSCCformer-f re-encodes every block from channel-output feedback. None of them, therefore, lets a scheduler choose each user's length after encoding, from CQI alone.

**None compares prefix and per-rate codes inside a scheduled multi-user system.** Your result is that encoding once with a decoder per length is best on mean PSNR. It beats one model per CBR by 0.78 dB and 1.9 points (decoded) when a quarter of the slots are lost, and ties at the target. At equal budget, the decoder per length recovers about half of the 0.17–0.47 dB nesting penalty ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md); [NOTES.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/NOTES.md)). DeepJSCC-l found no difference between the two decoder designs on CIFAR-10; your measurement prices the decoder per length on a larger code. This is a system verdict on a known mechanism, DeepJSCC-l's, and should be claimed as one.

### No field trains a code on the channel its scheduler makes

Four of the searches looked for this: deep JSCC, semantic communication, analog transmission and multimedia. All found **no work that trains a learned code on the SNR distribution its own scheduler induces**, confirming the survey's earlier negative result. The mechanism itself is ordinary training matched to a channel distribution fixed outside the system (DRJSCC, CFA-JSCC and euJSCC, above).

The co-design neighbours all differ in kind. Mohajer et al.'s constrained-RL controller adapts power, coding rate and JSCC parameters together with link adaptation, but does not retrain the code ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11276-026-04165-9)). McDowell, Panebianco, Chakraborty and Sun claim the first cross-layer design that jointly optimises PHY semantic compression and MAC allocation. The full text confirms that their meta-learned transceiver is built to work across channel conditions without retraining, which is robustness, the opposite of specialisation ([arXiv 2608.28441](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28441)). It is trained over SNRs from −5 to 20 dB and ages up to 500 slots, conditioned by FiLM on the codelength and the SNR, and frozen before the scheduler's Q-functions are learned. The physical layer therefore never sees the scheduler's channel, and "jointly" means one objective solved layer by layer. Shisher et al. choose a feature length jointly with scheduling, from a fixed menu of predictors, one per feature length, trained offline (full text; [arXiv 2308.10094](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10094)). Huang, Li, Chiang and Katsaggelos adapt video sources jointly with resource allocation, rather than retraining a codec ([Princeton](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/joint-source-adaptation-and-resource-allocation-for-multi-user-wi/)). DeepMA learns the access scheme together with the code, but without scheduler-induced statistics ([arXiv 2303.11543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11543)).

The idea is new and its payoff small, and prior evidence explains why: an over-the-air study finds that fixed-SNR and random-SNR training perform about the same under Rayleigh fading ([arXiv 2405.13403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.13403)). Your results are +0.28 dB over a same-budget control and +0.08–0.12 dB beyond specialising by length. One retraining round reaches the fixed point, and the codes' κ tracks their scheduler's deep-fade rate ([PAPER.md](/home/user/JSCCwClaude/jscc/docs/PAPER.md)). These are best framed as a consistency result about the code–scheduler interface. They should be scoped to low mobility, because the lift keeps only 8% of itself at r = 0.3.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the paper is about. Its scheduler is an import: worked out for media in 2001, for multi-user video in 2010, for deadline jobs in 2018–2022 and for goal-oriented communication in 2023–2024. None of those lines could schedule a learned code, because none had a rule for what a slot is worth to one. The link abstraction, and above all its dependence on slot-varying training, is therefore the contribution everything else rests on. It is the interface that lets decades of decomposition theory reach a new class of codes, the role EESM played for OFDM. Importing the theory is a gain, not a concession, because it turns several of your measurements into confirmed predictions: the bounded total gap of the Lagrangian index, the plateau of the certainty-equivalent rule, the fragility of a Whittle index when reports say little, and the small payoff of matched training under Rayleigh fading. Confirmed predictions are stronger evidence than the numbers alone.

The same theory shows where the work is exposed, and the exposure is time-sensitive. The few places where your numbers and the theory disagree are the highest-value checks before submission, because each is either an artefact of the bound or a genuine finding worth its own paragraph. Priority is perishable on both genuinely new fronts: two 2026 preprints already apply EESM to semantic OFDM links without validating it, and McDowell et al. claim the first cross-layer PHY–MAC co-design. The first batch of full texts retired one risk by realising it, and found another. Hosseini and Panwar do plot a relaxation value, so the certificate is new evidence of a standard kind. And Hao, Xu and Tong own the Lagrangian index; their theorem asks for an exact dual and a randomized tie rule that your implementation does not yet have, and those two gaps are where the 30 dB shortfall should be looked for first. Fu and van der Schaar's multi-user paper (arXiv 0903.0207) is the largest unread risk. The second batch found the nearest scheduler of a learned code, McDowell et al., and confirmed that it lacks your arm, because its slots carry fresh codewords that do not accumulate. It also traced the mechanism of contribution (6) to DeepJSCC-l, and gave contribution (1)'s training result a mechanism. The two 2026 EESM preprints are now the main unread risk to the abstraction claim. The third batch narrowed the stale-report claim. A relaxation bound that holds under partial channel observation is standard for belief-state restless bandits (Ouyang, Eryilmaz and Shroff plot one against N), so what stays yours there is the report model inside a deadline program and the stopping result. It also gave the PI index its exact form, which makes the PI-type baseline a few lines of code. The fourth batch answered the expected referee question on the digital side: Steiner and Shamai's layered HARQ is the competitor without transmit CSI, and its gains depend on conditions your digital chain does not share, except at the low report correlations where a two-layer check remains worth running. It also showed that the conflict rule matters because of your deadline: random conflict resolution is provably fine for age of information (Tang et al.), not for a terminal all-or-nothing target.
